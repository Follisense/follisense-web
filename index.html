Hair types

Every hair type has its own way of going wrong

A scalp under braids and a scalp under a blow-dry fail differently. Pick yours and see what FolliSense actually watches for.

Straight hair shows the scalp, for better and worse

Oil reaches the ends within a day and the part is visible from the moment you make it. That cuts both ways. You will notice a change early, and you will also notice a normal shed and worry about it. A record tells the two apart.

Wavy hair falls between two routines and often gets neither

Too much product and the waves go flat and the scalp goes greasy. Too little and the ends go dry while the roots stay oily. Most wavy scalps are dealing with buildup and an uneven balance at the same time, which is hard to diagnose by feel alone.

Curls cover the scalp, so an unhappy one goes unnoticed

Gel casts, refresh sprays and a wash every week or two leave residue that builds quietly under the curl pattern. By the time it itches enough to notice, it has usually been there a while.

The tightest patterns carry the most tension and the longest gaps

Braids, cornrows and wigs put steady pull on the hairline and nape, and a style stays in for weeks at a time. Tension and buildup are slow, cumulative and almost invisible day to day, which is exactly the sort of change a record is for.

Locs are a long commitment, and the scalp underneath is hard to see

A retwist that is a little too tight is fine once. Every six weeks for three years is a different question, and the perimeter is usually where it shows first. Photographs from the same angles make that visible long before a mirror does.

How it works

Three steps, then it runs on your rhythm

Set a starting point once, then check in when it makes sense for the way you wear your hair. Everything after that compares against what you already logged.

Your record

Six check-ins tell you what one never could

One rough scalp day is noise. The same symptom turning up in four of your last six check-ins is a pattern, and that is the sort of thing worth showing to somebody qualified.

FolliSense counts what you logged and shows it back plainly. It does not score you, rank you against anyone else, or tell you what it thinks is wrong.

Matched comparison

Two photos only sit side by side if they are actually comparable

Most hair photos taken weeks apart are useless next to each other. Different light, different point in the wash week, hair wet one time and dry the next. FolliSense records those conditions at the shutter and only offers a pair when they match. If nothing matches yet, it says so rather than showing you a comparison that means nothing.

For men

A cut every week counts too

Most hair apps have nothing for men beyond a hairline photo. FolliSense tracks the parts that actually repeat: the barber chair, the shave, the line-up, and what your scalp does in between.

Cuts and barber visits

Log a fade, a line-up or a full cut and check in around it, so you are comparing the same scalp at the same point after a cut rather than whenever you remember.

Razor bumps and irritation

Bumps, ingrown hairs and soreness after a shave get logged as their own thing, so you can see whether they follow a particular barber, blade or product.

Hairline and crown

Guided photos from the same angles each time, so a receding line or a thinning crown is something you can see side by side instead of guessing at in the mirror.

Fades & tapersLine-ups WavesLocs & twists Bald & shavedGrowing it out

Any scale describing hairline or crown change is a way of recording what you can see, not a diagnosis or a prediction. Hair loss has many causes. If you are worried about it, see a doctor or dermatologist.

Reminders

The style has been in longer than you think

Tell FolliSense when a style went in and how long you plan to keep it. You get a nudge as takedown week approaches, and again if it runs past what you planned, because four weeks has a way of quietly becoming seven.

The same works for retwists, wash days, colour appointments and barber visits. Reminders are built from what you told us, and they are a convenience rather than a rule. If a style feels tight, itchy or sore, do not wait for a notification.

Being clear
